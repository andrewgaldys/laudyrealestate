Las Olas Isles

Canal-front isles at the foot of Las Olas Boulevard.

At a Glance

  • Dredged in the 1920s
  • Drawbridges only to the inlet
  • Private security patrol

Character

Las Olas Boulevard began in 1917 as a dirt road across the wetlands between downtown and the beach. In the 1920s, developers dredged those wetlands into the Las Olas Isles: long, narrow “finger” islands separated by canals, a layout modeled on Venice. The 1926 hurricane stalled the boom, and the first luxury waterfront homes followed in the 1930s.

South of the boulevard, the Las Olas Isles Homeowners Association represents more than 300 households on nine isles and streets, including Isle of Capri, Coconut Isle, Bontona and Royal Plaza Drive, and funds a 24-hour private security service. North of the boulevard, Hendricks Isle and Isle of Venice follow the same pattern of canal-front lots.

The canals open onto the Intracoastal Waterway. The route to Port Everglades passes drawbridges, not fixed bridges: the 17th Street bridge from the isles south of the boulevard, and the East Las Olas bridge as well from those to the north. Depth, canal width and dock rules still vary from lot to lot; the Waterfront guide covers what to check.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • No fixed bridges between the canals and the inlet.
  • Las Olas Boulevard’s shops and restaurants at the end of the street, and the beach just across the Intracoastal.
  • An organized homeowners association with private security.

Cons

  • Drawbridges open on a schedule, so a trip out can mean waiting for the next opening.
  • On narrower lots, side setbacks limit the length of boat you can dock. Check the survey and zoning for the specific lot.
  • Like every canal neighborhood, flood zone, elevation and seawall condition need checking before you buy.
