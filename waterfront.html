Step 1

Start With the Boat

Four numbers rule out most of the market before a single showing.

  • LOA

    Length Overall

    Must fit the berth after setbacks

  • Beam

    Width

    Must fit the lift and the canal

  • Draft

    Fully Loaded

    Against depth at mean low water

  • Air Draft

    Highest Fixed Point

    Against every fixed bridge

Step 2

Fixed vs. Bascule

The waterway matters more than the neighborhood name.

Fixed Bridge
One clearance. If you don't fit at high tide, the house doesn't work.
Bascule
Opens on schedule. Costs time, never eligibility.
New River Rail
Rests open, closes for trains. 4 ft closed.
Coral Ridge
Intracoastal canals: no fixed bridges. Middle River: fixed bridges.
Schedules
Set in 33 CFR 117. Check the Local Notice to Mariners.

Step 3

Route Sets the Buyer Pool

Ranked from the deepest pool of buyers to the smallest.

  1. No BridgesAny boat the dock can hold. Harbor Beach is the benchmark.
  2. Bascules OnlyAny air draft. Count the openings.
  3. New River, Above the Rail BridgeAny air draft, plus train delays.
  4. Behind a Fixed BridgeCapped by clearance. Value tracks the height.

Inlets & Speed

Port Everglades
Deepwater inlet. The main exit for most of the city.
Hillsboro Inlet
Natural, shallower, sensitive to sea state.
Speed Zones
Measure in minutes. ICW idle zone near Port Everglades: Nov 15 – Mar 31.

Step 4

Frontage & Depth

100 feet on the plat rarely means 100 feet of berth.

Dock Reach
25 ft or 25% of waterway width, whichever is less. ULDR §47-19.3.
Example
60-ft canal → 15-ft dock.
Setbacks
Side setbacks cut usable length.
Wide Water
Easier turns, longer views, more wake.
Depth
Mean low water at the dock, canal mouth and route.

Step 5

Seawall Integrity

Broward County Code, Ch. 39, Art. XXV. The City of Fort Lauderdale sets its own minimum; confirm it with City engineering.

  • 4 ft

    Minimum Before 2035

    NAVD88, if built to reach 5 ft

  • 5 ft

    Required by 2050

    NAVD88

  • 50%

    Substantial Repair

    Of shoreline length or barrier cost. Triggers full compliance.

Red Flags on a Showing

  • Cracked or separating cap
  • Gaps or rust at panel joints
  • Sinkholes behind the wall
  • Leaning or bowing
  • Exposed rebar
  • Water over the cap at king tide

Step 6

What Moves Price

In order. The house comes last.

  1. Route to the OceanSets the ceiling on boat size.
  2. Usable FrontageWidth of the water and the berth.
  3. DepthAt mean low water.
  4. SeawallA failing wall is a deduction at replacement cost.
  5. Elevation & Flood ZoneInsurance, lending and the FEMA 50% rule.
  6. The HouseOnly after the water.

Before You Buy

Due Diligence

  • Boat specs in writing
  • Route map with every bridge
  • Survey with seawall cap (NAVD88)
  • Elevation certificate & flood zone
  • Depth soundings at low water
  • Marine contractor assessment
  • Seawall, dock & lift permits
  • Wind & flood insurance quotes
  • HOA or deed dock limits
  • Current bridge status

Quick Answers

  • "No fixed bridges" means any boat fits?

    No. Depth, canal width and dock limits still decide.

  • Is Intracoastal frontage fastest to sea?

    Not always. Bascules and slow zones add time.

  • Should I avoid an older seawall?

    Not if it's sound and high enough. Price a failing one as capital cost.
