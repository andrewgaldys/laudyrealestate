How Nick works

The approach is the same for buyers and sellers: measure what decides value before debating what does not.

  1. Define the requirement

    For buyers: vessel dimensions, how you will use the water, walkability, lot size, and timing. For sellers: the objective, whether that is price, speed, certainty, or privacy.

  2. Screen the water first

    Bridges on the route, controlling depth, usable frontage under city rules, and seawall elevation are checked before a showing, so tours are spent on homes that already fit.

  3. Price with matched comparables

    Waterfront comparables are matched on route, depth, frontage, and wall condition, not only on square footage and bedrooms.

  4. Verify with the right professionals

    Surveys that report seawall cap elevation in NAVD88, marine engineering inspections, permit searches, elevation certificates, and insurance quotes, gathered early enough to shape the offer.

  5. Negotiate from documented findings

    Adjustments tied to a survey, an inspection, or a permit record hold up better than adjustments tied to opinion.

For sellers, the same work becomes a pre-listing water profile: route and bridge data, depth, dock permits, and seawall documentation that answer buyer questions before they reach the negotiating table.
