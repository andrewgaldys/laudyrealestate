Approach

An analytical approach to waterfront property

Waterfront property is priced on physical facts that are easy to check and expensive to get wrong. Nick establishes those facts before discussing price, and he shows his work. The full method is laid out in the Fort Lauderdale waterfront guide.

For buyers

  • Rule properties in or out by bridge route, depth and dock geometry before scheduling tours.
  • Request the documents that matter early: survey, elevation certificate, permit history, seawall assessment, and condominium inspection and reserve reports.
  • Build comparable sales on the same bridge profile and frontage, then adjust for the house.
  • Name the deal-breakers plainly, including the inconvenient ones.

For sellers

  • Document what buyers pay for (route, clearance, frontage, depth, seawall height) so it is marketed rather than discovered.
  • Surface likely inspection issues before listing, while there is still time and choice.
  • Price against waterfront sales that genuinely compare, and explain the reasoning.

On every property

  • No guessing. When a clearance, depth or seawall elevation matters to a decision, Nick says how to verify it and who can.
  • Listing claims are treated as starting points, not facts.
  • Numbers come with their source and date.
