Harbor Beach

A gated enclave between the Intracoastal and the ocean.

At a Glance

  • Developed from 1942
  • Guard-gated entrances
  • One drawbridge to the inlet

Character

Harbor Beach was among the first Fort Lauderdale developments after the 1920s boom. The Harbor Beach Company, formed by George W. English, bought about 200 acres from pioneer Tom M. Bryan in 1940 and put its first 73 lots on sale in January 1942, behind a wall and gate designed by Miami architect Russell T. Pancoast. The Harbor Beach Surf Club, a private beach club, was founded the same year.

The neighborhood sits between the Intracoastal Waterway and the ocean, just north of the 17th Street Causeway. Its entrances have staffed gatehouses, and the Harbor Beach association pays for private security patrols and contracts extra patrols from the Fort Lauderdale Police Department. The Surf Club, for members only, keeps a 300-foot private beach and a marina.

The isles and lagoon open onto the Intracoastal just north of the 17th Street bridge, so boats reach Port Everglades through a single drawbridge.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • A short run to Port Everglades, with one drawbridge between the canals and the inlet.
  • Staffed gatehouses and patrols, funded by the association.
  • The Intracoastal on one side and the ocean on the other, with a private beach club for members.

Cons

  • Getting out can be slow: residents report trouble merging onto A1A at busy times and when the 17th Street bridge opens.
  • Security and amenities are paid for through association dues, and Surf Club membership is separate.
  • One of the city’s most exclusive neighborhoods, with prices to match.
