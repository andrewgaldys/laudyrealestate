Victoria Park

Walkable streets just northeast of downtown.

At a Glance

  • Platted in 1925
  • More than a square mile
  • Next to downtown

Character

Washington, D.C. developer Alfred G. Kuhn platted Victoria Park in 1925 and named it for his daughter, Victoria. The original subdivision covered about 24 blocks, with curving and diagonal streets and Lake Stranahan worked in to create scenic lots. Victoria Courts was added in February 1926; eight of its buildings are now designated city landmarks.

Today Victoria Park stretches more than a square mile, from Federal Highway east to Lake Stranahan and from Broward Boulevard north to Sunrise Boulevard. Its civic association, founded in 1980, is one of the city’s largest, and the neighborhood is known for its mature tree canopy and its position directly next to downtown, close to Las Olas and the beaches.

Most of the neighborhood is inland. A smaller number of lots back onto Lake Stranahan and the canals along its eastern edge. The neighborhood has grown from its original cottages into an upscale community, with older and newer homes side by side.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Walk or bike to downtown and Las Olas.
  • Mature trees and historic buildings, including Victoria Courts.
  • Most lots are inland, so most homes come without seawall and dock upkeep.

Cons

  • Few waterfront lots; keeping a boat usually means a marina or a different neighborhood.
  • Federal Highway, Broward Boulevard and Sunrise Boulevard are busy along the edges.
  • Ongoing rebuilding means old cottages and new homes sit side by side, and the look changes from street to street.
