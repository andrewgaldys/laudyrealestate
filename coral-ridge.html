Coral Ridge

Established streets from Sunrise to Oakland Park Boulevard.

At a Glance

  • Association since 1949
  • About 1,350 homes
  • Middle River to the Intracoastal

Character

Coral Ridge is one of Fort Lauderdale’s post-war neighborhoods. Its homeowners association dates to 1949, and it covers the land from East Sunrise Boulevard north to East Oakland Park Boulevard, between the Middle River and the Intracoastal Waterway.

It is mostly single-family: about 1,350 houses, plus some duplexes, condominiums and townhouses. Bayview is the neighborhood’s public elementary school. George English Park, on Sunrise Key, has a small-boat launch, and the Coral Ridge Yacht Club is in the neighborhood. The Galleria sits to the south and the Coral Ridge Mall to the north, and the beach is a five- to ten-minute drive.

Canals reach in from both the Middle River and the Intracoastal. From the Intracoastal side, boats reach Port Everglades through three drawbridges: Sunrise Boulevard, East Las Olas and 17th Street. On the Middle River side, check the route and every bridge on it before you buy.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • A large, established single-family neighborhood with an active association.
  • Shopping at both ends, from the Coral Ridge Mall to the Galleria.
  • A public boat launch at George English Park, and a yacht club in the neighborhood.

Cons

  • Three drawbridges between the Intracoastal canals and the inlet make trips out slower than from neighborhoods farther south.
  • Middle River lots have a different route, depth and set of bridges than Intracoastal lots.
  • Sunrise Boulevard, Oakland Park Boulevard and Federal Highway carry heavy traffic along the edges.
