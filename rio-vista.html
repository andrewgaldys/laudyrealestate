Rio Vista

Between the New River and the Intracoastal, minutes from downtown.

At a Glance

  • Platted in the 1920s
  • Over 1,000 homes
  • One drawbridge to the inlet

Character

Rio Vista is one of Fort Lauderdale’s oldest neighborhoods. Mary Brickell recorded the first plat of the area, and after her death C.J. Hector bought the land for his “River View” development. By February 1923, the Fort Lauderdale Herald reported that Rio Vista was booming, with more than 5,000 feet of sidewalk laid and streetlights installed. The 1926 hurricane ended the boom, and building picked up again after World War II.

The city defines Rio Vista as the area from US-1 east to the Intracoastal Waterway, and from the New River south to SE 12th Street. It has more than 1,000 homes on tree-lined streets with sidewalks, and a city architectural survey documented 29 of its buildings as potential historic landmarks.

Waterfront lots sit on the New River and on the canals of the isles along the Intracoastal. From those canals, boats reach Port Everglades through one drawbridge, at 17th Street.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Next to downtown and Las Olas, with Federal Highway along the west edge.
  • Water on two sides, and a single drawbridge between the isles and the inlet.
  • Older architecture: the city has documented 29 potential historic landmarks here.

Cons

  • Those landmarks are potential, not designated, so older homes nearby can be torn down and replaced.
  • Federal Highway and SE 17th Street traffic along the edges.
  • Older homes often mean older seawalls, roofs and systems. Inspect carefully.
