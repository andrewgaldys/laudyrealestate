How waterfront value works here

What sets value on Fort Lauderdale's water

Two homes on the same street can sit in different markets. In Fort Lauderdale, a waterfront property's buyer pool is set less by square footage than by five physical facts. Nick confirms each one before recommending an offer or a list price.

  1. 01

    Route to the ocean

    Which inlet, how many bridges, and how many minutes at posted speeds. Port Everglades is the deepwater inlet for most of the city; owners farther north may weigh the Hillsboro Inlet instead.

    How ocean access is evaluated →
  2. 02

    Bridge clearance

    A fixed bridge sets a hard limit on your boat's air draft and removes every buyer whose boat doesn't fit. A bascule (draw) bridge costs time instead, on a published opening schedule.

    Fixed vs. bascule bridges explained →
  3. 03

    Usable frontage

    Frontage on a plat is not usable dock length. Canal width, side setbacks and the City's limits on how far a dock may project into the water decide what you can actually tie up.

    How frontage and dock limits work →
  4. 04

    Depth at the dock

    Listings rarely state depth at mean low water, which is the number that matters to a keel or running gear. Canal mouths and turning basins can shoal; soundings settle the question.

    Checking depth along the route →
  5. 05

    Seawall height & condition

    Broward County's tidal flood barrier standard ties new and substantially repaired seawalls to minimum elevations. A wall that needs replacement is a capital cost the price should reflect.

    Seawall rules and inspection points →
  6. →

    Then the house

    Only once the water is accounted for do interiors, architecture and finishes enter the comparison. That order is how buyers on the water actually spend, and how Nick builds comparable sales.

    How these variables move price →

Who Nick works with

Five kinds of clients, five different first conversations

The right first question depends on why you're here. Each group below has its own set of variables, and its own short list of what to send Nick so the first reply is useful.

Waterfront buyers with specific boating requirements

If the boat came first, the search starts with it. Nick filters by bridge route, depth and dock geometry before looking at a single interior, which usually removes most of the market in an afternoon.

Send Nick

  • Length overall, beam, loaded draft and air draft
  • Your current boat and the one you expect to own next
  • How you use it: sandbar days, offshore fishing or Bahamas crossings

Luxury and waterfront sellers

Pricing a waterfront home means pricing its water. Nick documents the attributes buyers pay for, such as a bridge-free route, frontage, depth and seawall elevation, so they appear in the marketing rather than being discovered during inspection.

Send Nick

  • The address and your target timing
  • Work already done to the seawall, dock, roof and systems
  • Your objective: highest price, speed, certainty or privacy

Relocating from the Northeast

Moving from New York, New Jersey, Connecticut or Massachusetts means learning a different set of carrying costs. Florida has no state personal income tax, but property insurance, flood insurance and condominium reserves work differently than they do up north. A homestead exemption, filed with the Broward County Property Appraiser, also brings the Save Our Homes cap, which limits annual increases in assessed value to 3% or the change in CPI, whichever is lower. Confirm your situation with a tax advisor.

Send Nick

  • Where you're moving from and your target move date
  • Work, school or family anchors that shape location
  • How you want to spend a typical weekend

Oceanfront and Intracoastal condominium buyers

Since Florida's 2022 condominium safety legislation, many buildings three stories and taller must complete milestone structural inspections and structural integrity reserve studies. Those documents, not the lobby, now drive future assessments and should be read before you write an offer.

Send Nick

  • Budget, preferred stretch of beach or Intracoastal
  • Boat slip, parking, pet and rental requirements
  • Whether this will be a primary or seasonal residence

Investors and builders

On much of Fort Lauderdale's water, the lot carries the value. Nick evaluates renovation versus rebuild economics against FEMA's substantial-improvement rule, seawall replacement cost and zoning, before a purchase price is set.

Send Nick

  • Lot criteria: frontage, water width, bridge profile
  • Hold period and whether you plan to build
  • Target return or exit price

Method

How a property gets evaluated

These are the questions a well-advised buyer's surveyor, inspector and marine contractor will ask. Asking them first protects price on either side of the transaction.

  1. Map the route

    Every bridge to the inlet, its type and published closed clearance, and transit time at posted speeds, measured against your boat's air draft and your tolerance for waiting on openings.

  2. Measure the water

    Seawall length, canal width, the maximum distance a dock may project, side setbacks, lift capacity, and depth at mean low water at the dock and along the route.

  3. Read the wall and the grade

    Seawall cap elevation in NAVD88, visible condition, permit history, finished-floor elevation and FEMA flood zone. Each one feeds insurance cost and future capital spending.

  4. Price against true comparables

    Comparable sales are only comparable when the water is. Nick groups sales by bridge profile, frontage and depth first, then adjusts for the house.
