Waterfront real estate

Look beyond the waterfront label.

Boating access, usable frontage and improvement value can change the answer to the same question: what is this property worth to you?

The six checks behind a waterfront comparison

A home’s asking price becomes more meaningful when the water, land and improvements are evaluated separately.

  1. 01

    Actual navigable route

    Map the route from the dock to open water. Confirm fixed bridges, bridge clearances, tide effects, canal width and turning room for the vessel you expect to use. “Ocean access” can describe very different boating experiences.

  2. 02

    Usable frontage and dockage

    Linear waterfront footage is a starting point. Check the shape of the lot, setbacks, dock dimensions, mooring restrictions, depth at the dock and whether any permitted structures match what is on site.

  3. 03

    Seawall and maintenance exposure

    Ask for permits, repair history and a qualified inspection where appropriate. A future seawall project can affect cost, construction timing and the practicality of a renovation.

  4. 04

    Lot value versus the house

    Separate the value of land from the value of the current improvement. For a teardown, study recent land oriented sales and the cost and time to deliver a new home. For a finished home, test the quality and age of the build against replacement alternatives.

  5. 05

    Comparable sale adjustments

    Compare similar locations and water access first. Then examine differences in frontage, lot size, bridge conditions, age, condition and sale timing. A 75 foot and 100 foot waterfront lot should not be treated as interchangeable simply because they are nearby.

  6. 06

    Carrying and approval costs

    Review insurance availability, flood risk, property taxes and any approvals required for the intended use. Verify with the appropriate insurer, surveyor, engineer, marine contractor and city or county office before committing capital.

Waterfront FAQ

Does “no fixed bridges” guarantee my boat fits?

No. Confirm the full route, water depth, dock dimensions and vessel requirements. Conditions can differ along the same canal.

Is more frontage always worth a premium?

Frontage can improve dock flexibility and lot utility, but its value depends on shape, location, access and what recent comparable buyers paid.

Can I rely on an online flood or insurance estimate?

Use it to start a discussion. Obtain property specific documents and quotes before using a number in your budget.
